United States of America

An Overview of Physical Geography and Earth Processes

Historical Context

The physical geography of the United States has influenced its human geography. Native tribes established their own distinctive societies according to North America's diverse environments, long before any European influence.

After Europeans colonized America and formed their nation in 1776, the westward expansion during the 19th century was influenced by natural geography, such as rivers like the Mississippi River and passes through mountains like the Appalachians and Rockies.

Cultural Profile

American culture is a complex amalgamation with influences ranging from indigenous cultures, through European settlement, African culture, to immigration from around the world. Regional cultural distinctions depend on geography, from coastal maritime culture to Midwest agricultural culture.

The landscape plays an important role in American literature, arts, and folklore, helping create the first national park system in the world, established in Yellowstone in 1872.

Social Structure

With a population of 340 million and more, the US is one of the most urbanized countries in the world. Population distribution in the US is dense along coastal regions, the Great Lakes basin, and large river basins.

Demographics and patterns of human settlement are still changing in relation to geographic conditions, among other factors.

Economic Profile

The U.S. is home to the world’s highest nominal GDP owing to technology, services, manufacturing, finance, and agriculture. Physical geography plays an important role in providing a basis for this economic activity.

Extensive fertile plains offer a lot of food production, extensive mineral and energy resources facilitate industrialization, and extensive natural ports along the Atlantic, Pacific, and Gulf Coasts allow trade.
