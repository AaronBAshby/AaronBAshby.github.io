Maps, Location, and Cartographers

Geographic coordinates, spatial orientation, map projections, and cartographic history of the United States.

Geographic & Historical Framework

1. Continent & General Location

The United States is located on the North American continent. The contiguous United States is located in the central portion of the continent, neighbor to Canada to the north, the United Mexican States and the Gulf of Mexico to the south, the Atlantic Ocean to the east, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. Additionally, the nation has Alaska in the far northwestern corner of North America and the volcanic archipelago of Hawaii in the central Pacific Ocean.

2. Latitudinal Range

The conterminous United States spans a latitudinal range of approximately 24°30' N (Ballast Key/Key West, Florida) to 49°23' N (Lake of the Woods, Minnesota along the 49th parallel border).

When taking all 50 states into account, the range expands significantly from south to north: starting at 18°55' N at Ka Lae (South Point), Hawaii, and reaching 71°23' N at Point Barrow (Utqiaġvik), Alaska, crossing from the sub-tropics into the Arctic Circle.

3. Historical Existence & Sovereign Eras

The United States declared its national sovereignty on July 4, 1776, with the adoption of the Declaration of Independence, and received formal international recognition under the Treaty of Paris in 1783. Prior to sovereign nationhood, the territory was inhabited for millennia by diverse indigenous nations and subsequently colonized by European powers (primarily Great Britain, Spain, France, and the Netherlands) throughout the 17th and 18th centuries. The United States of America has existed continuously as a constitutional federal republic from 1789 to the present.

Famous Cartographer: John Wesley Powell (1834–1902)

John Wesley Powell was an American geologist, geographer, Civil War veteran, and pioneering cartographer who shaped scientific mapmaking and land management in the United States. In 1869, Powell led the famous Powell Geographic Expedition, embarking on a three-month boat voyage down the Green and Colorado Rivers through the unmapped canyons of Utah and Arizona, producing the first topographic and structural survey of the Grand Canyon region.

Serving as the second Director of the United States Geological Survey (USGS) from 1881 to 1894, Powell established the foundational system of standardized topographic quadrangle mapping across the entire United States. His visionary work, Report on the Lands of the Arid Region of the United States (1878), argued that western state boundaries and property maps should be drawn according to natural hydrologic drainage basins rather than arbitrary geometric grid lines, anticipating modern watershed management and spatial cartography.
